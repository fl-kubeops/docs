S26CS060  FYP  Phase I (RS)  Page v Abstract  
 
Green  Guard is an intelligent plant health monitoring system to help farmers identify plant problems 
more accurately using deep learning and feature level fusion techniques. Traditional plant disease 
detection systems mainly rely on leaf images only and often ig nore important factors like 
environmental conditions and historical plant data. Because of this, they may not provide complete 
or accurate results. The proposed system solves this issue by combining three different types of data 
like plant leaf images, env ironmental data and temporal plant data.  
 
The system uses deep learning models to analyze each type of data separately. A Convolutional 
Neural Network (CNN) model extracts visual features from leaf images, an Artificial Neural Network 
(ANN) model processes environmental information such as tempera ture, humidity, soil moisture etc  
while a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) model studies historical trends and changes over time. 
These extracted features are then combined using a feature fusion layer to create a complete 
understanding of plant health. The fused features are then processed  using an ensemble of Multi 
Layer Perceptron (MLP) models to simultaneously detect plant diseases, nutrient deficiencies and 
environmental stress conditions.  
 
The project operates in the agricultural domain where biological and environmental factors define 
the problem context, while all data processing, modeling and prediction are performed using 
computer science and AI based methodologies.  
 
The main goal of Green  Guard is to provide a smart, accurate, and reliable plant monitoring system 
for modern farming. The system will also generate useful recommendations for farmers like 
improving irrigation, applying fertilizers or controlling diseases. This project contribut es to the fields 
of artificial intelligence, smart agriculture, and precision farmin g by introducing a multimodal and 
multi task learning framework for better plant health diagnosis.

1. Introduction and Background  
1.1 Product Vision & Problem Context  
Problem Description:  
Agriculture plays a major role in food production and the economy, but farmers often face serious 
challenges in identifying plant diseases, nutrient deficiencies, and environmental stress conditions at 
an early stage. [2] Traditional methods of crop monitoring mostly depend on manual inspection by 
farmers or agricultural experts, which can be time consuming, expensive, and inaccurate. [12] 
Traditional observation and experience are widely used in agricultural practices, leading to incorrect 
decisions in many cases, without the presence of smart systems. For instance, plant diseases are 
typically only detected once they become noticeable on l eaves rather than through an automated 
system. Fertilizer application is frequently based on guesswork and not good soil nutrient analysis, 
which results in over or under usage. Likewise, irrigation is applied on a fixed schedule or based on 
intuition rath er than actual soil moistures data, that can result in water waste or crop stress. Farmers 
are also unable to distinguish similar plant diseases without expert assistance during their work. 
These constraints emphasize the need for intelligent solution, suc h as Green Guard.  
 
Literature Survey:  
 
Deep learning (DL) has emerged as a powerful approach in modern agriculture by enabling accurate 
analysis of complex image and environmental data, supporting applications such as crop yield 
prediction, weed detection, and plant disease identification [6].  
 
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are widely used for analyzing leaf and crop images to detect 
diseases, pests, and nutrient deficiencies by extracting spatial features efficiently [9], while Recurrent 
Neural Networks (RNNs) model time series agricultur al data including environmental changes and 
sensor readings to predict stress conditions over time [7]. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) process 
structured data such as soil nutrients and weather parameters to identify hidden patterns affecting 
plant heal th [8]. Ensemble learning integrates CNNs, RNNs, and ANNs to enhance prediction 
accuracy, robustness, and generalization by combining visual and non -visual data for more reliable 
and actionable crop monitoring systems [10].  
 
Previous research applied deep learning for early leaf spot disease detection using CNN for feature 
extraction and RNN based for classification on the PlantVillage  dataset (nearly 20,000 images). 
Results showed based models achieved higher accuracy than traditional machine learning methods 
[11]. This study evaluated only a single CNN architecture, relied solely on image data, and did not 
compare multiple deep learni ng or ensemble approaches.  
 
Another study proposed an ensemble framework combining CNNs and RNNs to analyze leaf images 
with time series environmental data. Ensemble techniques such as majority voting and weighted 
averaging improved prediction accuracy on the Kaggle Cotton  dataset [12].  
 
A separate study used CNNs to learn unsupervised features for 44 plant species and applied 
deconvolutional visualization for feature interpretation. CNN based features outperformed traditional 
handcrafted techniques [13]. The research focused only on speci es identification, not on disease 
detection, nutrient deficiency, or integrated plant health monitoring using ensemble models.

Proposed Solution:  
Green Guard is a proposed multi modal, multi task deep learning system, which combines several 
Artificial Intelligence methods for the monitoring of plant health. It employs a type of neural network 
called a convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract vi sual features from leaf images, such as 
spots, lesions, discoloration, and disease textures. Structured environmental and sensor data, such as 
temperature, humidity, soil moisture, pH and nutrient levels are processed by Artificial Neural 
Networks (ANNs) t o discover hidden patterns that influence plant health. In the case of sequential 
and historical data, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) models are used to model the temporal 
dependencies and environmental trends over time.  
Independent feature extraction is followed by a feature level fusion technique in which the image 
feature vector, environmental and temporal feature vectors are concatenated and then input to a dense 
fusion layer to learn cross modal interaction and adapti vely identify feature importance. An ensemble 
learning approach is then adopted, which is composed of two MLP based models (shallow and deep) 
to enhance the robustness and generalization of the system by inputting the same fused 
representation.  
Finally, the results of both models are fused by using the average voting ensemble method to get 
stable predictions for several tasks, such as disease detection, nutrient deficiency identification and 
stress analysis. The system can also do multi task lear ning with multiple output heads, allowing 
prediction of multiple types of agricultural conditions from the same representation.  
The system is developed with a focus on user friendly and low literacy friendly interface, using simple 
visual indicators, icons and color coded alerts as the mediums. This means that even semi literate or 
illiterate farmers can be able to understand the s tatus of the plants, results of the disease detection 
and what needs to be done without having to be technical.

User Research:  
 
Observation  of Current Agricultural Process at Dogar Farm : 
 
To understand how crop monitoring and plant health management work in the real world, our FYP 
team conducted a field observation study. Since our project, Green   
Guard, aims to solve actual practical issues using technology, we needed to see exactly how small to 
medium scale farmers manage their crops, notice diseases, and keep track of environmental 
conditions without automated systems.  
 
 Field Details : 
 
• Location:  “Dogar Farms” near railway station Kasur  
• Type:  Typical semi  commercial small to medium scale farm environment.  
• Crops Observed:  Tomatoes, potatoes, and green leafy vegetables  
• Participants:  Farm supervisor (15+ years experience) , field workers  
• Observation Timing:  Early morning farming activities, when crop inspection and field 
work are most active.  
• Duration:  Several hours of routine farm operations (crop checking, watering, and 
maintenance tasks).  
 
Scenario  and Ground Observations : 
 
During our time at the farm, we walked alongside the workers to see their daily routine. The process 
of checking crop health was entirely manual. The laborers walked slowly between the crop rows, 
looking down at random plants to see if anything looked unus ual. If a leaf appeared yellow, spotted, 
or wilted, they would stop to inspect it closer.

Observation  of Solution at FYP Student Led Research & Innovation Event:  
 
The observation phase was conducted during participation in the FYP Student Led Research & 
Innovation event, where different agriculture related monitoring challenges, smart farming ideas, and 
practical crop management issues were discussed and analyzed. D uring this activity, observations 
were made regarding the limitations of traditional plant monitoring approaches and the increasing 
need for intelligent agricultural systems.  
 
The observation revealed several important issues faced in agricultural environments:  
• Farmers mainly rely on manual inspection for detecting plant diseases and stress conditions.  
• Early symptoms of nutrient deficiencies and environmental stress are often difficult to 
identify visually.  
• Environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, and soil moisture significantly affect 
plant health but are rarely analyzed together with visual symptoms.  
• Existing systems mostly focus only on image based  disease detection and ignore multimodal 
agricultural data.  
• Lack of intelligent monitoring systems may lead to delayed diagnosis, reduced crop quality, 
and productivity loss.  
These observations helped define the core problem addressed by Green  Guard and supported the 
development of a multimodal AI based agricultural monitoring framework.  
 
The proposed system received encouraging feedback for its multimodal approach and relevance to 
smart agriculture  multimodal learning . The project also received appreciation for addressing real 
world agricultural challenges through computational techniques.

S26CS060  FYP  Phase I (RS)  Page 11 2. Agile Framework Setup  
The Green Guard project will be developed in an iterative fashion, with effective collaboration and 
continuous improvement, by following the Agile software development methodology as outlined 
below in the Scrum framework  
The project will be broken up in small sprints of development, with each sprint dealing with a 
functional piece of the system, such as pre  processing  the datasets, identifying diseases, analyzing 
the environment, building the dashboard, or generating alerts. All epics, user stories, and tasks will 
be kept in the team's product backlog. Sprint planning meetings will be held for the purpose of 
choosing t asks, establishing sprint goals, and assigning sprint tasks. Weekly Scrums will be used to 
monitor progress, review what has been accomplished, clarify issues encountered, and enhance team 
coordination.  
Sprint review and sprint sessions will take place after each sprint to discuss and reflect on what has 
been accomplished, find areas for feedback and input, and plan for the next iteration. Backlog 
management, sprint tracking, Kanban Boards, task organizat ion will be achieved using agile project 
management tools like Notion The goal of the team is to continuously test, deliver the system on 
time, and be flexible by applying Agile and Scrum practices .

sprint cycle defined for the project.  
1. Daily Scrum Activities  
Daily Scrum activities are conducted to ensure continuous progress tracking and coordination among 
team members. Due to the academic schedule and distributed working environment, a hybrid 
communication approach is adopted.  
Approach:  
 
• The team maintains daily progress updates through a dedicated communication channel  
• Updates are also reflected in the Notion Scrum board for transparency and tracking.  
• Each team member provides a structured update including:  
• Tasks completed since the last update . 
• Planned tasks for the current day . 
• Any technical or implementation blockers . 
Frequency:  
 
• Daily  
• Short synchronization meetings: 2  times per  sprint (10–15 minutes)  when required  
 
Objective:  
 
• Ensure continuous visibility of project progress  
• Identify and resolve blockers early  
• Maintain alignment across development tasks  
 
2. Sprint Review Process  
Sprint Review meetings are conducted at the e very 2 week sprint to evaluate completed work and 
demonstrate functional deliverables.  
Approach:  
 
• Each team member presents completed sprint tasks based on assigned responsibilities:  
• Backend and frontend modules  
• AI/ML model development and integration  
• Data preprocessing and evaluation components  
• Functional demonstrations are conducted using GitHub repositories, or Notion 
documentation updates.  
 
Participants:  
 
• Development Team  
• Product Owner

implemented in Notion. This ensures transparency, continuous progress monitoring, and effective 
collaboration among team members . 
Task Tracking Methodology : 
 
All project tasks are managed using a Notion Kanban board, which provides a visual workflow for 
tracking development progress across all modules of the system.  
 
The Kanban system is divided into the following task states:  
• To Do:  Tasks that are planned and not yet started .  
• In Progress:  Tasks currently being actively developed .  
• Testing:  Tasks under validation, evaluation, or debugging .  
• Done:  Fully completed and verified tasks .  
 
Workflow Structure : 
 
The project follows a hybrid task assignment model, where:  
• Tasks are assigned based on module ownership (Frontend, Backend, AI/ML)  
• Team members can assist each other across modules when required .  
• This ensures flexibility while maintaining accountability .  
 
Tool Used : 
 
The following tools are used for task tracking and collaboration:  
• Notion: Primary tool for task management and progress tracking .  
• GitHub: Used for code version control and integration tracking .  
The Notion board is accessible to all team members and is also shared with the supervisor for real  
time project monitoring and evaluation.

dataset preparation, development, testing, and deployment.

• Sprint  wise task tracking using Agile Scrum .  
• Effort points assigned based on workload and complexity .  
• Burndown chart updated after every sprint .  
• Tracks completed vs remaining project effort .  
 
Expected Burn Rate : 
 
• Early sprints have moderate burn rate due to research and planning activities .  
• Development and integration sprints consume higher effort points .

S26CS060  FYP  Phase I (RS)  Page 42 5.5 Backlog Refinement Plan  
 
Backlog refinement in Green Guard  is a sprint  based activity used to keep the product backlog 
updated, prioritized, and aligned with ongoing development of the AI  based plant monitoring system.  
 
Frequency : 
 
• Conducted once per sprint (every 2 weeks) .  
• Updated after each sprint review based on feedback .  
• Minor updates can be made anytime in Notion .  
 
Process : 
 
• Reprioritize tasks  based on sprint progress and AI pipeline needs .  
• Refine user stories  by adding missing details and acceptance criteria .  
• Adjust story points  based on actual development effort .  
• Split large tasks  into smaller sprint ready tasks .  
• Update backlog  using sprint results, model performance, and supervisor feedback .  
• Maintain tracking  using Notion and GitHub commits .
